The Ultimate Full Body Workout

Train smarter, build balanced strength, and make every workout count.

What Is a Full Body Workout?

A full body workout is a strength training routine that targets multiple major muscle groups in a single session. Instead of focusing exclusively on your upper or lower body, full body workouts combine exercises for your legs, chest, back, shoulders, arms, and core.

At Form & Flow Fitness, we believe full body training is one of the most practical ways to build strength while maintaining a flexible schedule. Whether you're a college student, a busy professional, or someone getting back into exercise, this routine is designed to make your time count.

Your Workout Overview

Duration: Approximately 45 minutes

Fitness Level: Beginner to intermediate

Equipment: Dumbbells and an exercise mat

Sets: 2 sets per exercise

Repetitions: 8–12 per set unless otherwise specified

Rest: 60–90 seconds between sets

Frequency: 2–3 times per week with recovery between sessions

Your 45-Minute Full Body Workout

Begin with five minutes of light movement, such as marching in place, arm circles, and bodyweight squats. Then complete the following exercises with controlled movements and proper technique.

1. Goblet Squats

Muscles Worked: Quadriceps, glutes, and core

Hold one dumbbell at chest height with your feet shoulder-width apart. Bend your knees and hips to lower into a squat, then press through your feet to return to standing.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 10

2. Dumbbell Romanian Deadlifts

Muscles Worked: Hamstrings and glutes

Hold two dumbbells in front of your thighs. With slightly bent knees, push your hips backward and lower the weights along your legs. Return to standing by engaging your glutes.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 10

3. Dumbbell Chest Press

Muscles Worked: Chest, shoulders, and triceps

Lie on your back on an exercise mat with your knees bent. Hold a dumbbell in each hand and press upward. Slowly lower your upper arms toward the floor before repeating.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 10

4. Bent-Over Dumbbell Rows

Muscles Worked: Back and biceps

Hinge forward at your hips while maintaining a neutral spine. Pull the dumbbells toward your hips, squeeze your shoulder blades gently, and lower the weights with control.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 12

5. Dumbbell Shoulder Press

Muscles Worked: Shoulders and triceps

Start with dumbbells at shoulder height. Press them upward while keeping your core engaged. Lower slowly to the starting position without excessively arching your back.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 10

6. Reverse Lunges

Muscles Worked: Quadriceps, glutes, and core

Step one foot backward and bend both knees to lower your body. Push through your front foot to return to standing. Alternate legs while keeping your balance.

Sets and Reps: 2 × 8 per leg

7. Forearm Plank

Muscles Worked: Abdominals and deep core muscles

Place your forearms on the floor with your elbows under your shoulders. Extend your legs behind you, engage your core, and hold a straight body position while breathing normally.

Sets: 2 × 30 seconds

Benefits of Full Body Workouts

Efficient Training: Work multiple muscle groups without needing separate gym sessions for every body part.

Balanced Strength: Develop your upper body, lower body, and core together.

Flexible Scheduling: A full body routine can fit into two or three weekly training days.

Beginner-Friendly: Simple movement patterns make it easier to learn foundational strength exercises.

How Often Should You Do Full Body Workouts?

For many beginners, two or three full body workouts per week provide a manageable starting point. Scheduling recovery days between demanding strength sessions gives your muscles time to recover.

As your strength improves, you can gradually increase your weights, repetitions, or sets. Remember that proper form and consistency matter more than lifting the heaviest weights possible.
