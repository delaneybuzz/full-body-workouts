15-Minute Ab Workout

A stronger core starts with small, consistent movements.

Build Core Strength at Home

A strong core supports everyday movement, stability, and exercise technique. Your core includes more than just your visible abdominal muscles. It also includes deeper muscles that help stabilize your spine and pelvis.

This simple ab workout requires no equipment beyond a comfortable exercise mat and can fit into a busy schedule.

Workout Duration: Approximately 15 minutes

Equipment: Exercise mat

Difficulty: Beginner-friendly

Structure: 2 rounds of 5 exercises

Rest: 15–30 seconds between exercises and 60 seconds between rounds

Your Core Circuit

1. Dead Bugs

Targets: Deep abdominal muscles and core stability

Lie on your back with your arms reaching upward and knees bent at 90 degrees. Slowly extend your right arm and left leg while keeping your lower back stable. Return to the starting position and switch sides.

Repetitions: 10 per side

2. Reverse Crunches

Targets: Rectus abdominis

Lie on your back with your knees bent. Use your abdominal muscles to gently curl your pelvis upward, bringing your knees toward your chest. Lower your hips slowly without swinging your legs.

Repetitions: 12

3. Forearm Plank

Targets: Entire core

Place your forearms on the floor with your elbows below your shoulders. Extend your legs behind you and maintain a straight line from your head to your heels. Keep breathing throughout the hold.

Duration: 30 seconds

4. Bicycle Crunches

Targets: Abdominals and obliques

Lie on your back with your hands lightly supporting your head. Bring one knee toward your chest as you rotate your opposite shoulder toward it. Alternate sides using controlled movements rather than momentum.

Repetitions: 20 total

5. Side Plank

Targets: Obliques and lateral core stability

Lie on one side with your elbow directly below your shoulder. Lift your hips and hold your body in a straight line. For an easier variation, keep your bottom knee on the floor.

Duration: 20 seconds per side

How Often Should You Train Abs?

Start with two or three core sessions per week. You can perform this workout on its own or add it after a full-body strength session. Allow recovery when your muscles feel especially fatigued or sore.

Core Training Is About More Than Appearance

Ab exercises strengthen your abdominal muscles, but they cannot selectively remove fat from your stomach. Core training works best as part of a balanced routine that includes full-body strength training, cardiovascular activity, recovery, and supportive nutrition.
